Explanations

Each explanation method answers a different question

MethodIts questionSession
Permutation importanceHow much worse does the model do without this feature?1
Partial dependence, ALEHow does the prediction change as this feature changes?2
Shapley valuesHow much did each feature move this one prediction?3
None of them asks what causes default. All three describe the model, on data that may have features moving together.

Recap from DS-204 Day 2: built-in split-gain importances say what the trees used in training, not what matters on new data. Today's three methods are model-agnostic or exact, and each fails in its own way with correlated features. Tools are interchangeable: the scikit-learn inspection module, the SHAP library, DALEX; the labs use the course functions and LightGBM's own TreeSHAP.

Permutation importance

Shuffle one column on the holdout and measure how much worse the predictions get

def rise_one(df, col, rng):
    d = df.copy()
    d[col] = d[col].to_numpy()[rng.permutation(len(d))]
    return log_loss(y, predict(d)) - base
  • On the holdout: importance for new applicants, not memorised noise.
  • On the validator's model: Amana with the Platt repair, log loss 0.284.
  • One shuffle is one draw: repeat and report the spread.

The rise is in log loss, a proper score, so importance means "how much worse the probabilities get". Ask: what does a negative importance mean? Shuffling helped, by chance: the feature carries nothing measurable.

Sources: scikit-learn user guide, permutation feature importance.

The correlated pair

Shuffling one income creates applicants who do not exist

0.84
correlation of log declared income and log bank inflow
36%
of rows outside the real declared-to-inflow range after shuffling declared income
  • 98% of real applicants declare between 0.72 and 2.36 times their bank inflow.
  • Shuffle declared income alone and a third of rows declare 20,000 against an inflow of 4,000.
  • The model reads that as overstatement and predicts high risk: importance measures its reaction to fiction.

This is the extrapolation problem named by Hooker, Mentch and Zhou: unrestricted permutation forces the model to predict outside the data. Overstatement is a real risk signal in these data (the generator plants it), so the model is right to react; the importance number is simply not about the feature alone.

Sources: Hooker, Mentch and Zhou, Unrestricted Permutation forces Extrapolation.

Partial dependence

Partial dependence sets everyone's value to the same number and averages

for v in grid:
    d = sample.copy()
    d["declared_income"] = v      # everyone
    pd_curve.append(predict(d).mean())
  • Answers: what if every applicant had declared v, all else unchanged?
  • Fine when the feature is independent of the others.
  • With bank inflow fixed, a high v means overstatement for most applicants.

The individual curves behind the average are ICE curves (the stretch). The question PD answers is a counterfactual about the model's input, which is exactly where correlated features create rows that never occur.

Sources: scikit-learn user guide, partial dependence and individual conditional expectation.

Accumulated local effects

ALE moves each applicant only a little, inside their own bin

1 · BinCut declared income at the grid values
→
2 · NudgeMove only that bin's applicants from its lower to its upper edge
→
3 · AccumulateAdd the mean changes from left to right, centre at zero

Every prediction is for a nearby, realistic applicant: their bank inflow already matches an income in that bin.

ALE was proposed by Apley and Zhu to fix exactly this: effects of correlated predictors without extrapolation. Its price: it answers a local question ("how does risk change between similar real applicants?"), and it is harder to explain to a committee than PD.

Sources: Apley and Zhu, Visualizing the Effects of Predictor Variables in Black Box Supervised Learning Models.

Shapley values

Shapley values split one prediction exactly among the features

  • Each feature's value is its average contribution over all orders of adding features.
  • For trees it is computed exactly and fast (TreeSHAP).
  • They add up: base value plus the features' values equals the raw score of that applicant.
v = amana.predict(X10, pred_contrib=True)
# columns: one per feature, then the base
raw = amana.predict(X10, raw_score=True)
np.allclose(v.sum(axis=1), raw)   # True

LightGBM computes TreeSHAP itself; the SHAP library's TreeExplainer gives the same numbers and nicer plots. The values are on the log-odds scale of the raw model, before the Platt repair; a monotone map after the model does not change which features push risk up.

Sources: Lundberg and Lee, A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions; LightGBM documentation.

From the panel to a rule

Show a reason only when it survives refits

Proposed rule

Show a reason when it is the top reason in at least four of five refits; group correlated features into one reason; otherwise say the model has no stable main reason.

Why it matters

An applicant told a reason that flips with a refit has been given an accident of training, not an explanation.

Lab 3.3 step 5. The SDAIA AI Ethics Principles put transparency and explainability to those affected among the principles, especially for outcomes that may harm them. A refused loan is such an outcome. The principle does not specify a stability test; the rule here is the validator's recommendation, labelled as such.

Sources: SDAIA AI Ethics Principles.

A change request

Version 2.2 adds mobile-wallet months and gains 0.004 of AUC

  • Rasil's team proposes one new feature: how long the applicant has used a mobile wallet.
  • Holdout AUC: 0.757 without, 0.761 with.
  • Age is excluded from the model on purpose, and remains so.

The validator's question

What does the new feature carry, and who will be told it is their reason?

Both models are refits with seed 1 on all training rows, so the comparison is fair. A gain of 0.004 is within what a refit alone can change. Ask the room to guess which applicants have the shortest wallet histories.

Proxies

A proxy is a permitted feature that carries an excluded one

  • Wallet months correlates with age band at -0.44: median 43 months for 18 to 24, 12 for 55 and over.
  • Age is related to default in these data, so the model learns to use what wallet months carries of it.
  • "Uses no age" stays true as written and becomes misleading in effect.
What goes wrong. Checking only the feature list. Claim 3 of the card passed Lab 1.1 on exactly that check.

The documented misleading result

A caution names what the feature carries, who receives it and what to do

Wallet months correlates with age (r = -0.44) and is a top-three reason for 47% of applicants over 55 against 21% of those aged 18 to 24. Shown to officers or applicants, "short wallet history" would in effect tell older applicants that their age counts against them, in a model that excludes age.
Recommendation. Do not add the feature for an AUC gain of 0.004; if it is added, do not show it as a reason, and audit by age band.

Lab 3.4 step 5. This is the "one documented misleading result" that outcome LO3 asks for. Mitigation of such effects is DS-309's subject; the validator's job is the evidence and the caution.
