The brief

Validate one package independently and defend a fit-for-use decision

  • Package. Amana-R (top-up loans) or Amana-F (farm loans) from labs/capstone/, or a peer's model with its card, data and a later batch.
  • The week, repeated. Recompute the claims, calibration, intervals and sets, explanation stability, the subgroup table, the later batch, the checklist.
  • The decision. Fit, fit with conditions or not fit, with owned conditions and named human review.

Submission

A link to a public GitHub repository holding the validation notebooks, the run logs, and a README that reports calibration, coverage, explanation stability, subgroup findings and the decision.

The course functions in labs/mizan.py work on both packages: pass the package folder to U.model and read its CSV files directly, as Lab 4.4 does. A peer's package needs the same parts; agree it with the trainer by the end of Day 4. The data are synthetic, so public repositories are fine.

How the validation is scored

Five criteria, 100 points

Calibration and coverage
25%
Explanations
20%
Subgroup and shift findings
25%
Decision
20%
Reproducibility
10%
Full marks. Every finding has a number, an interval where one applies, and the file that produced it.

What full marks look like. Calibration and coverage: before-and-after scores, coverage by group with defaults counted. Explanations: grouped importance and a stability panel over refits. Subgroup and shift: the cause of the later batch's change found and argued, gaps tagged with the guide. Decision: every high finding answered by an owned condition. Reproducibility: a clean clone runs and reproduces the README's numbers.

The defence

Eight minutes before the committee, then four minutes of questions

2 min · the package

What it claims, and which claims failed on your numbers.

4 min · the findings

The high findings, each with its evidence; the later batch's cause.

2 min · the decision

The decision, the conditions and their owners.

The panel plays the committee. In the questions it picks one finding and you rerun the cell that produced it.

Also expect "why not approve it as it is?" and "who will review these applicants?". Panel questions that separate strong from weak: Which of your findings would you drop if challenged, and why? What would change your decision? Show me the cell that produced this number. If the later batch's cause was not found, a clear account of what was ruled out still scores.
