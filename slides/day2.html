Session 1

Conformal intervals

  • Compute the split conformal threshold with its finite-sample correction
  • Wrap a fitted regression in intervals and check coverage on new rows
  • State the one assumption the guarantee rests on
01
The problem

A loss prediction of 12,000 riyals says nothing about how wrong it can be

  • Amana's loss model predicts the riyals lost if a loan defaults.
  • Finance provisions on it; collections plan on it.
  • A point prediction is silent about its error: 12,000 could mean 11,000 to 13,000 or 2,000 to 30,000.

What the validator adds

An interval around every prediction that contains the real loss 90% of the time, with the condition under which that holds.

Ask Talib's question: "If I provision for the predicted loss on 100 defaults, how many will cost more than I planned, and by how much?" A point prediction cannot answer it. The loss model is trained on defaulted loans only, so its holdout has 993 rows, not 10,000.

The one assumption

Conformal prediction needs only one thing: new rows drawn like the calib rows

  • Exchangeable: the calib rows and the new row could be shuffled without changing their joint distribution. Independent draws from one population are exchangeable.
  • No assumption on the model, the shape of the errors or the features.
  • The guarantee breaks when the population moves: a new product, a new region, a shift in incomes.
What goes wrong. Quoting "90% guaranteed" for applicants who no longer look like the calib rows. Session 4 measures how much is lost.

This is the third row of yesterday's assumption table. Keep the definition informal: if you could not tell which row is the new one by looking at the joint distribution, the rows are exchangeable. Time order breaks it (later rows differ), and so does any selection of who applies.

Sources: Angelopoulos and Bates, A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction.

Split conformal, in three steps

Score the calib rows, take a quantile, widen every prediction by it

1 · ScoreOn calib defaults, the error |loss - prediction|
→
2 · ThresholdThe ceil((n+1)(1-α))-th smallest score
→
3 · Intervalprediction ± q, clipped at zero
No retraining. The model is used as delivered; only its predictions on calib rows are needed.

Press once per step. Step 1: the score is how badly the model did; any score works, the absolute residual is the simplest. Step 2: with 895 calib defaults and alpha 0.1, the rank is ceil(896 x 0.9) = 807. Step 3: every holdout loan gets the same plus or minus q. The fact that the validator never opens the model is what makes this a validation tool.

Why "n + 1"

Counting the new loan turns "about 90%" into "at least 90%"

def conformal_quantile(scores, alpha=0.1):
    s = np.sort(scores)
    k = ceil((len(s) + 1) * (1 - alpha))
    if k > len(s):
        return inf          # too few rows
    return s[k - 1]
  • The new loan's score is equally likely to fall at any of the n + 1 ranks.
  • So the chance it falls at or below rank k is k / (n + 1), at least 90%.
  • With 8 calib rows, 90% needs a ninth: the interval is infinite. Honest, and a warning about small groups.

The lab checks the infinite case: with 8 scores and alpha 0.1, k = 9 > 8. Return to it in Session 3: a group-conditional threshold for a group with few calib rows suffers the same way.

Coverage is a promise about the average over draws, not about one interval

  • One calib set can give 87% or 93% on one test set.

02
Classification

An officer who sees only the top grade cannot see the model's doubt

  • Amana's grade model gives three probabilities: on time (A), late but repaid (B), default (C).
  • The top grade is right for 74.4% of holdout applicants.
  • For some it is a near-certainty; for others three grades are close. The top grade looks the same.

A prediction set

The grades the model cannot rule out, chosen so the true grade is in the set 90% of the time. Its size is the doubt.

Grades in the holdout: A 72%, B 18%, C 10%. Ask: would Fuad's officers treat an applicant whose set is {A} the same as one whose set is {A, B, C}? They should not, and today they cannot tell them apart.

The simplest sets

LAC keeps every grade whose probability clears one bar

score = 1 - P_cal[i, true_grade]     # calib rows
q = conformal_quantile(score, 0.1)    # 0.834
in_set = P_hold >= 1 - q              # 0.166
  • Score: one minus the probability of the true grade.
  • On Amana: a grade enters the set if its probability is at least 0.166.
  • Smallest sets on average; can be empty.

LAC stands for least ambiguous set-valued classifier. It is MAPIE's default for classification ("lac"). Its sets are the smallest that keep the marginal guarantee, which is why it spends its errors on the hard cases.

Sources: Angelopoulos and Bates; MAPIE documentation.

Adaptive sets

APS adds grades from most to least likely until their total reaches the threshold

Sortgrades by probability
→
Accumulateadd the next grade's probability
→
Stopwhen the total passes q (with a random share of the last grade)
  • Unsure applicants, whose probabilities are spread, get larger sets.
  • The randomisation makes coverage exact rather than conservative.

APS is from Romano, Sesia and Candes. The course helper implements the randomised version with a fixed seed so runs repeat. Without randomisation APS over-covers, sometimes badly with three classes: the threshold can land at 1 and every set holds all three grades.

Sources: Romano, Sesia and Candes, Classification with Valid and Adaptive Coverage.

Session 3

Coverage by group

  • Measure coverage and width by subgroup
  • Explain why marginal coverage says nothing about a group
  • Apply group-conditional (Mondrian) conformal and read its cost in width
03
Marginal and conditional

90% for everyone together can be 77% for the loans that matter

  • Marginal coverage: over all loans, 90%. This is what split conformal guarantees.
  • Conditional coverage: within a group (large loans, a region), 90%. Not guaranteed.
  • One width for all loans is too wide for loans whose losses vary little and too narrow for those that vary a lot.

The question at Mizan

Talib provisions large loans separately. He needs their intervals to hold, not the average.

Ask the room to predict which loans the single width under-covers before showing the table. Most will say large loans; ask why. Losses are a share of the loan, so their spread grows with the loan. The data generator plants exactly that, and adds extra spread in the North.

The repair

One threshold per group gives each group its own guarantee

def group_thresholds(s, groups, alpha=0.1):
    return {g: conformal_quantile(
                   s[groups == g], alpha)
            for g in sorted(set(groups))}
  • Called group-conditional or Mondrian conformal.
  • Thresholds: large 13,256, medium 4,332, small 1,536 riyals.
  • Each group needs enough calib rows: 79 small-loan defaults give a threshold from 79 scores.

The groups must be defined before looking at the holdout and known for a new applicant (loan size is known at application). Calib defaults per band: medium 476, large 340, small 79. With finer groups (band by region by age) the cells empty out and thresholds become infinite.

Session 4

Coverage under shift

  • Simulate a covariate shift by reweighting a held-out sample
  • Measure the coverage lost for intervals and for sets
  • Explain why the guarantee needs exchangeability and what restores it
04
Coverage under shift

When applicants change, the interval does not know

  • A conformal threshold is a fact about the calib rows.
  • If next year's applicants earn more, borrow more or come from a new region, the rows are no longer exchangeable.
  • The interval keeps its width and quietly loses coverage.

At Mizan

A planned campaign for salaried applicants with higher incomes. Higher incomes mean larger loans.

Covariate shift: the mix of applicants changes, the relation between features and losses does not. It is the gentlest shift, and it still breaks the guarantee where it moves the errors. Day 4 shows a harsher kind, a broken data feed.

A shift you control

Tilt the holdout towards higher incomes and measure what is lost

z = standardise(log(declared_income))
w = exp(strength * z)
shifted = hold.iloc[rng.choice(n, n, p=w / w.sum())]
  • Strength 0: the holdout as it is.
  • Strength 1: median declared income of defaulters from 9,400 to 16,100 riyals.
  • The truth of every resampled row is known, so coverage can be measured exactly.

Simulation by reweighting is the validator's stress test: no new data needed, and the shift is chosen to match a plausible business change. Name the business change in the report, so the stress has a meaning.

When the shift is known

Weighting the calib scores by the shift restores the coverage

83.2%

93.6%

  • Each calib score counts as much as its row is more likely after the shift.
  • Needs the weights: in a simulation you know them; with real applicants you estimate them.
  • The guarantee then rests on that estimate.

The stretch of Lab 2.4. Weighted conformal prediction is from Tibshirani, Barber, Candes and Ramdas. Present it as an option for the developers when a planned change is known in advance, not as the validator's default.

Sources: Tibshirani and others, Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift.
