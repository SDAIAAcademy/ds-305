Model drift or a broken input?

The guide's Model Drift (p. 66) and Concept Drift (p. 28) describe a changing world. This is Measurement Bias (p. 63): values recorded too low.

Every real investigation has a decoy: something else that changed at about the same time. The test is whether it explains the pattern: a cause must explain why the failure is in one channel and starts in one month. Retraining here would teach the model the broken unit and hide the fault.

The stretch writes the range check: bounds at the reference period's 0.1th and 99.9th percentiles; it alerts on the first 40 March app applications and stays quiet in February. That check is part of condition C2 this afternoon.

Training and serving features

PSI compares different applicants; a replay compares the same applicant twice

Training path

The job that built the training table, run again on the source records.

Serving path

The values the scoring service received and logged on the day.

  • Drift: different applicants, different values; the two paths agree.
  • Skew: the same applicant gets different values from the two paths.
  • Replay: take scored applications, rebuild them through the training path, compare column by column with a tolerance fixed before looking.

A distribution test cannot tell a changed population from a changed pipeline, because the rows differ between the two periods. A replay holds the records fixed, so any difference beyond the tolerance is in the code or the feed. Ask: what tolerance for money that one path rounds to tens? The course uses 0.5% for amounts in riyals and exact agreement for counts.

03
Checklists

A checklist turns "is it documented?" into twelve answerable items

  • Each item is present, partial or missing, with the file and field that decide it.
  • Two validators reach the same score; the developers know what to send.
  • The course list adapts REFORMS (reporting standards for science that uses machine learning) and model cards to a credit model.

Three parts

Conceptual soundness (S1 to S5), outcomes (O1 to O5), monitoring (M1, M2): the three elements of validation in SR 11-7.

SR 11-7 (US Federal Reserve and OCC supervisory guidance on model risk management) names conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring and outcomes analysis as the elements of validation; it is a widely used frame, not a Saudi requirement. REFORMS is Kapoor and others, Science Advances 2024. Model cards are Mitchell and others, 2019.

Sources: SR 11-7; REFORMS; Model Cards for Model Reporting.

04
The fit-for-use decision

The committee needs one of three answers, and the conditions that go with it

Fit

Use as documented. Rare for a first submission.

Fit with conditions

Use only while named conditions hold, each with an owner.

Not fit

Do not use. Say what would change the answer.

The decision is Dr. Sameera Al-Fadda's committee's; the validator drafts it with the evidence. SDAIA's National AI Risk Management Framework gives entities a method for identifying, assessing, treating and monitoring AI risks; conditions with owners and monitoring are how a validation report puts that into practice for one model.

Sources: National AI Risk Management Framework, executive summary (SDAIA); SDAIA AI Ethics Principles.

Human review

Name which applicants an officer must see, and give the officer the evidence

A slogan

"Borderline cases are reviewed by a human."

A rule

Every application from the Northern region, aged 18 to 24, or with all three grades in its 90% set.

Automation Bias (guide p. 19): officers who see only a score defer to it. They need the grade set, stable reasons, authority to decide differently, and their overrides monitored.

The grade set from Day 2 earns its place here: an application whose 90% set holds all three grades is one the model cannot place. Ask the room how many applications the rule sends to officers: the North is 5% of holdout applicants and 18 to 24 is 12%, so about a sixth before the full sets are added; Fuad's team must be able to absorb it, and the report should state the share.

Sources: SDAIA AI Bias Reference Guide.

  • A condition without an owner is a wish; a review rule without a list of applicants is a slogan.

Day 4 · three things to keep

What a validation report gives the committee

Supported gaps

By group, with intervals, tagged with the guide and rated by a rule stated before looking.

Causes, not symptoms

Slice, inputs, reasons, then a replay: a broken feed is not a degraded model.

Owned conditions

A decision the committee can enforce, with monitoring and real human review.
